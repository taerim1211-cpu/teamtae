If you've read the post on [securities-backed lines of credit](/blog/securities-backed-lines-of-credit-explained/),
you already know most of what a **pledged asset line** is. "Pledged Asset Line" is the name Charles
Schwab uses for its SBLOC, and other firms sell the same kind of loan under their own names. The
label changes from brokerage to brokerage. The structure underneath is mostly the same.

So instead of repeating that post, this one focuses on two things: what **pledging** an account
actually means, and the related **pledged-asset mortgage**, where investments stand in for a down
payment on a house.

## What "pledging" means

When you open a pledged asset line, you move investments into a separate **pledged account** and
sign an agreement giving the lender a legal claim on it. Three things change:

- **You can usually still trade inside it.** You can sell one fund and buy another, as long as the
  account keeps enough collateral value.
- **Withdrawals are restricted.** You can't move money or securities out if that would leave the
  loan under-collateralized.
- **The lender can sell.** If the account's value falls too far and you don't add collateral or pay
  down the loan, the lender can sell holdings to cover it, as with a
  [margin loan](/blog/margin-loans-explained/).

Like any SBLOC, a pledged asset line is a **non-purpose loan**. You can't use it to buy more
securities, and retirement accounts can't be pledged.

## Comparing the brand names

Since every firm uses its own name, compare offers on the terms, not the label:

- **Minimum line size and account size.** Many start around $100,000 in eligible assets.
- **Rate:** usually a benchmark like SOFR plus a spread. Ask how the spread changes with the size of
  the line.
- **Advance rates** for the assets you actually hold, especially concentrated stock positions.
- **Collateral call rules:** how much time you get to respond, and whether the lender chooses what to
  sell.
- **Fees:** setup, annual or early-closure fees.
- **Where the account has to be.** Most require the pledged assets to stay at that firm.

## The mortgage twist: pledged-asset mortgages

A related product uses pledged investments in place of a **down payment**. With a pledged-asset
mortgage, you borrow most or all of a home's price and pledge a portfolio as extra collateral
instead of putting cash down. The lender gets the protection a down payment would give, and you
keep your investments.

People use it to:

- **Avoid selling appreciated stock** and paying capital gains tax to raise a down payment
- **Avoid private mortgage insurance (PMI)**, which conventional loans usually require with less
  than 20% down
- **Stay invested** instead of moving a large amount into a house

The pledge isn't permanent. Lenders typically release the pledged assets once the loan balance falls
enough relative to the home's value, through paydown or appreciation. Requirements vary by lender.

## A worked example

Say you're buying an **$800,000** home. A 20% down payment would be **$160,000**.

- **Selling stock for the down payment:** if those shares were bought for $60,000, you'd realize a
  $100,000 gain. At a 15% long-term capital gains rate, that's **$15,000** in federal tax, and more
  in many states.
- **Pledging instead:** you borrow the full $800,000 and pledge investments as collateral. Lenders
  usually require more than $160,000 in stock, because stock prices move. Say they ask for
  **$250,000**. You owe no tax now, and the portfolio keeps growing.

The trade-off: you're paying interest on an extra $160,000 of mortgage debt, and both your house and
your portfolio are now tied to the same loan.

## The risks

- **Two kinds of collateral at risk.** If the market drops, the lender can require more collateral or
  sell pledged securities, even if you've never missed a mortgage payment.
- **Bad timing.** Market crashes and recessions tend to arrive together. A falling portfolio can mean
  a collateral call just when your job or income is also less secure.
- **Higher debt.** Borrowing 100% of a home's value means more interest and no equity cushion if home
  prices fall.
- **Less flexibility.** The pledged assets are locked until they're released. You can't easily move
  them to another broker or withdraw them in an emergency.
- **Sales incentives.** As with SBLOCs, firms earn money on these loans. Make sure the product fits
  your plan rather than the firm's.

## Who it suits

Pledged asset lines and pledged-asset mortgages make the most sense for people with a large taxable
portfolio full of appreciated assets, steady income, and enough cash or other assets to answer a
collateral call without selling. For most people buying a first home, a conventional down payment
is simpler and safer.

## The short version

A pledged asset line is an SBLOC under another name: a credit line backed by a pledged investment
account that you can use for nearly anything except buying more securities. Compare offers on rate,
advance rates, call rules and fees, not on the brand. The pledged-asset mortgage takes the same idea
further, letting investments replace a down payment so you avoid selling and PMI. Either way, you're
borrowing against assets that can fall, and the lender gets to sell them if they do.
