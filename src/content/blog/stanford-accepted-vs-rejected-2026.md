Stanford admits about 3.6% of applicants, one of the lowest rates in the country. With odds like that, almost everyone who applies has great grades and scores. So what set admitted students apart this year?

A tally of 136 self-reported 2026 results from r/collegeresults offers some clues: 34 admitted, 96 rejected, and 6 waitlisted.

## First, a caution

Students who post their results tend to be strong applicants, so 25% of this group got in, about seven times Stanford's real rate. With 34 admits, a difference of a few people could be noise. Treat these patterns as hints, not rules.

## A perfect GPA mattered more than the SAT

| | Admitted | Rejected or waitlisted |
|---|---|---|
| Share with a 4.0 (of those who shared GPA) | 73% | 45% |
| Median SAT | 1560 | 1550 |
| Median AP/IB courses | 13 | 11 |
| National-level award | 88% | 77% |

Nearly three in four admits who shared their GPA had a perfect 4.0, compared with fewer than half of everyone else. The SAT barely separated the groups: a 10-point difference between two very high medians. Admits also took about two more AP or IB courses.

## Admits started things

The activities that showed up more often among admits had something in common: initiative.

- **Community service:** 71% vs. 52%, the biggest activity gap
- **Founded an organization:** 53% vs. 35%
- **Research:** 74% vs. 59%
- **Published research or a paper award:** 26% vs. 12%

Some activities showed up more often among applicants who *weren't* admitted:

- **Music:** 24% vs. 40%
- **AP Scholar award:** 21% vs. 37%

That doesn't mean music or AP Scholar hurt anyone. AP Scholar is so common among strong students that it just doesn't set anyone apart. A fair reading is that Stanford's admits more often built something of their own, like a club, a nonprofit, or research that led to a paper.

## Applying early made a big difference

Stanford's early round is **Restrictive Early Action**. It isn't binding, but you can't apply early to other private colleges. In this sample, **10 of 21 early applicants were admitted (48%), compared with 11 of 61 in the regular round (18%)**.

Strong students are more likely to apply early, so this doesn't prove the early round itself helps. Still, the gap is large. Stanford also waitlisted very few people in this sample, so most applicants got a clear yes or no.

## Key takeaways

- **A 4.0 mattered a lot here.** The GPA gap was much bigger than the SAT gap.
- **High test scores are expected, not special.** Both groups had median SATs above 1500.
- **Show initiative.** Founding something, doing research that led to a paper, and steady service stood out.
- **If Stanford is your first choice, think about applying early.** The early deadline is November 1, and early applicants in this sample did much better.
