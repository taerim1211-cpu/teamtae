It's easy to picture NCAA academic eligibility as a ladder: Division I has the highest bar, Division II sits a little lower, and Division III is lower still. The first two parts are roughly right. The third isn't. Division III doesn't have a lower NCAA GPA line. For most athletes, it doesn't have one at all.

## D1 and D2: same machine, different setting

Divisions I and II run on the same system. Both require 16 NCAA-approved core courses, and both certify athletes through the NCAA Eligibility Center. Neither requires an SAT or ACT score anymore. The NCAA dropped test scores from initial eligibility in 2023.

The difference is the GPA line:

- **Division I:** 2.3 minimum core-course GPA
- **Division II:** 2.2 minimum core-course GPA

The rules for what counts as a core course are the same in both. Only English, math, science, social science, world language, comparative religion, and philosophy count. Your core-course GPA is calculated from those classes alone, so it can be very different from the GPA on your transcript.

Division I adds a couple of extra layers. The **10/7 rule** requires 10 core courses, including 7 in English, math, and science, to be done before senior year. Division I also has named tiers below the line. An athlete who clears 2.0 but not 2.3 becomes an **academic redshirt**: they can practice and receive a scholarship, but they can't compete their first year. Below that, they're a **nonqualifier**.

## D3: a different system entirely

This is the part that catches people off guard. Division III has no league-wide core-course GPA requirement for domestic athletes. Each school sets its own admission and academic standards, and the NCAA doesn't certify your grades. Domestic D3 recruits still create an account with the Eligibility Center to get an NCAA ID, but nobody checks their GPA there.

International athletes are the exception. If you're an international student heading to a D3 school, you do go through Eligibility Center certification before you can compete, and the process takes time.

## Why "D3 is easier" is the wrong takeaway

Because D3 has no NCAA GPA floor, its gate is simply each school's own admissions process. At many D3 schools, that's far more selective than D1's 2.3. MIT, Johns Hopkins, Amherst, and Williams all compete in Division III. Getting in there as an athlete is a much higher academic bar than clearing an NCAA minimum.

## What to do with this

- **Aiming for D1 or D2?** Track your core-course GPA, not your transcript GPA, and plan your schedule around the 10/7 rule early in high school.
- **Comparing D1 and D2?** A 0.1 GPA gap almost never decides it. Roster size, scholarship structure, and level of competition matter far more.
- **Considering D3?** Look up each school's academic profile the same way any applicant would. That's your real target.
- **International athlete?** Don't assume D3 lets you skip certification. Start the Eligibility Center process on the same timeline a D1 recruit would.

The key point: D1 and D2 are two settings on one system, and D3 is a different system altogether.
