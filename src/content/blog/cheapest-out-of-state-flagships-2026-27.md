Going to a public university out of state can cost almost as much as going to a private college. But the price varies a lot from one state's flagship to the next. CollegeIntel ranked 51 public flagships by 2026-27 out-of-state tuition and fees. The cheapest charges less than a quarter of what the most expensive one does.

## The 10 cheapest flagships

| Rank | University | Out-of-state | In-state |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | University of South Dakota | $13,616 | $9,916 |
| 2 | University of North Dakota | $17,479 | $12,289 |
| 3 | University of Wyoming | $25,392 | $8,532 |
| 4 | University of Oklahoma | $29,163 | $10,485 |
| 5 | University of Idaho | $29,300 | $9,824 |
| 6 | LSU | $29,643 | $12,966 |
| 7 | University of Alaska Fairbanks | $30,450 | $11,730 |
| 8 | Ole Miss | $31,168 | $10,378 |
| 9 | University of Nevada, Reno | $31,268 | $11,079 |
| 10 | University of Nebraska-Lincoln | $31,542 | $11,472 |

Only six flagships charge out-of-state students less than $30,000. The median is $38,800 (South Carolina).

## The most expensive

At the other end of the list, Michigan charges $67,096, Virginia charges $62,054, and UC Berkeley charges $57,484. Michigan and South Dakota are $53,480 apart each year. Over four years, that adds up to more than $210,000 before financial aid.

## The cheapest deals are the discounts

Several schools on the list charge some out-of-state students even less than the listed price:

- **South Dakota** charges the in-state rate ($9,916) to new students from 11 nearby states, including Minnesota, Illinois, Colorado, and Wisconsin, and to children of USD alumni.
- **North Dakota** charges Minnesota residents a lower rate of $13,535.
- **Idaho and Nevada** take part in the Western Undergraduate Exchange (WUE). Students from Western states pay $13,538 at Idaho and $15,770 at Nevada, less than half the regular out-of-state rate.
- **Alaska Fairbanks** plans to charge every new student the in-state rate starting in fall 2027, pending approval from University of Alaska regents in November 2026.

## How much you pay for not being a resident varies

The gap between in-state and out-of-state tuition varies a lot. At South Dakota, out-of-state students pay about 1.4 times the in-state rate. At Florida, they pay more than five times as much ($34,616 vs. $6,436), and UNC Chapel Hill is close behind ($49,593 vs. $9,351). Schools like these are designed mainly for their own state's residents, so out-of-state students make up the difference.

## Key takeaways

- **The cheapest flagships are mostly in the Plains and the West.** South Dakota, North Dakota, and Wyoming lead the list.
- **Look for exchange programs before the sticker price.** If you live near South Dakota or in a WUE state, you may pay much less than the listed rate.
- **These are tuition and fees only.** Housing, food, and travel aren't included, and some figures depend on credit load or major. Nebraska's business, architecture, and engineering courses cost more.
- **Prices are before financial aid.** Merit scholarships can change the comparison, so run each school's net price calculator.
