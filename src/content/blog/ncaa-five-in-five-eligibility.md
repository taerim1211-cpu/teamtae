For decades, NCAA eligibility ran on a familiar formula: five years to play four seasons. The redshirt year lived in the gap between those two numbers. On June 23, 2026, the Division I Cabinet voted unanimously to replace that with something simpler to state and very different in practice: five seasons of competition within a five-year window.

## What actually changed

The old model counted seasons. The new one runs a clock. The five-year window starts at whichever comes first: full-time college enrollment, or the academic year following an athlete's 19th birthday (if that birthday falls before September 1).

That shift takes a lot of machinery with it. Gone are:

- Season-of-competition limits
- Sport-specific eligibility rules
- The redshirt designation as a formal tool
- Eligibility-extension waivers

A few exceptions can still pause the clock: pregnancy, active-duty military service, and official religious missions. Outside those, the window runs.

## The age trigger is the real story

"Five seasons instead of four" sounds like a straightforward gift to athletes, and for many it is. But the more consequential part is the age trigger. Under the old system, a delayed start didn't cost anything by itself. Under the new one, the clock can start without the athlete being enrolled anywhere. Families planning a gap year, a prep year, or a late start should do the math on when the window opens, not just when college does.

## Who it applies to, and when

- **Fall 2027 enrollees and later:** fully governed by the new model.
- **Fall 2026 enrollees and current athletes with eligibility left:** schools apply whichever system benefits each individual athlete.
- **Athletes who finished through 2025-26:** no retroactive fifth season.

One thing that doesn't move at all: academic progress requirements. Credit-hour minimums and degree-completion benchmarks still apply on their own track, whichever eligibility model you're under. An extra season of competition doesn't come with extra room on progress-toward-degree.

## Still in court

The transition isn't fully settled. In *Wisne v. NCAA* in the District of Colorado, Judge Charlotte Sweeney granted a preliminary injunction on July 31, 2026, giving Class of 2022 athletes a fifth season. The Tenth Circuit stayed that injunction on August 21, 2026, and as of this fall the question is unresolved. Anyone on the edge of the cutoff shouldn't treat their status as final yet.

The practical takeaway: figure out exactly when your clock starts, don't count on a redshirt that no longer exists, and keep your academic progress on schedule regardless of how many seasons you have left.
