Going to a public university in another state usually means paying out-of-state tuition, which can be several times the rate for students who live there. But the listed out-of-state price isn't always what you pay. There are four main ways to get the in-state rate, or something close to it.

## 1. Schools that offer in-state rates to certain states

A few universities offer their in-state rate to students from other states.

- **University of South Dakota:** New students from 11 nearby states pay the in-state rate of $9,916 instead of $13,616. The states are Colorado, Illinois, Iowa, Kansas, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, Wisconsin, and Wyoming. Children of USD graduates qualify from any state.
- **University of Alaska Fairbanks:** It plans to charge every new student the in-state rate starting in fall 2027. That would cut tuition and fees from $30,450 to about $11,730. The plan still needs approval from University of Alaska regents in November 2026.

## 2. Deals between neighboring states

Some neighboring states agree to give each other's residents a discount. These deals are called reciprocity agreements.

- **Minnesota:** The University of Minnesota has agreements with Wisconsin, North Dakota, and the Canadian province of Manitoba. Students from those places pay less than the regular out-of-state rate.
- **North Dakota:** Minnesota residents pay about $13,535 at the University of North Dakota, compared with $17,479 for other out-of-state students.

## 3. Scholarships that remove the out-of-state charge

At some schools, winning a scholarship can lower your tuition to the in-state rate.

- **University of South Carolina:** Out-of-state students with a qualifying university scholarship pay the in-state rate of $6,144 per semester instead of $19,050. That saves more than $25,000 a year.
- **Texas public universities:** Texas lets its public universities charge the in-state rate to out-of-state students who win competitive scholarships. At Texas A&M, you need at least $4,000 a year in qualifying scholarships, and the state limits how many students can get this deal.
- **University of Arkansas:** Its Non-Resident Tuition Award covers 50% to 90% of the difference between in-state and out-of-state tuition, depending on the award level.

## 4. Regional tuition exchanges

These programs let students from one region pay reduced tuition at public colleges in other states in the same region.

- **Western Undergraduate Exchange (WUE):** Students from Western states pay no more than 150% of in-state tuition. Montana's WUE rate is $13,774, compared with its regular out-of-state rate of $34,894. Idaho, Nevada, and Hawaii also offer big discounts.
- **Midwest Student Exchange Program (MSEP):** Students from eight Midwestern states pay no more than 150% of in-state tuition in selected programs.
- **Academic Common Market:** Students from 15 Southern states can pay in-state tuition if they want a major that no public college in their home state offers. At some schools, such as Georgia and Auburn, it only starts in junior year.
- **New England Tuition Break:** New England residents pay a reduced rate in approved majors. At UConn, that's $30,592 instead of $44,242.

## Key takeaways

- **Check your home state first.** Most of these deals depend on where you live, so start with programs that include your state.
- **Read the fine print.** Many programs have GPA minimums, limit which majors qualify, or require you to apply by a deadline.
- **Get the rate in writing.** Make sure your discounted tuition shows up in your financial aid offer, not just on a website.
- **Compare total costs.** A school with a low regular out-of-state price can still beat an exchange discount somewhere else. Our post on [the cheapest out-of-state flagship universities](/blog/cheapest-out-of-state-flagships-2026-27/) lists the lowest regular rates.
