DECA looks like one activity from the outside, but it has 61 competitive events across six career clusters: Marketing, Finance, Business Management and Administration, Hospitality and Tourism, Entrepreneurship, and Personal Financial Literacy. What most of those events have in common is less obvious. They grade you on two very different skills, and students who prepare for only one of them tend to fall behind.

## The three event formats

Every DECA event falls into one of three broad formats:

- **Roleplays and case studies:** You take a multiple-choice exam for your career cluster. Then, on site, you get a business scenario, a short amount of prep time, and a judge to present your solution to.
- **Prepared events:** You submit a written project ahead of time and then deliver a prepared oral presentation on it.
- **Online simulations:** You compete in real time from a classroom instead of traveling to a conference.

Depending on the event, you compete alone, with one partner, or on a three-person team.

## Why the exam-plus-roleplay format is tricky

The exam measures how broadly you know business concepts. The roleplay measures something else: how comfortable you are thinking on your feet and explaining an idea clearly while a judge watches. A student who aces the exam but has never practiced improvising out loud will usually lose to someone who prepared for both halves.

That's the most useful thing to understand about DECA. The two parts need two separate kinds of practice. Study content for the exam. For the roleplay, practice working through scenarios under a time limit, out loud, as many times as you can. Reading about a case in your head doesn't build the same skill as presenting one.

## How students advance

Competition happens in three levels:

1. **Local chapter competitions** at your school
2. **Chartered association conferences** at the state or provincial level
3. **The International Career Development Conference (ICDC)**

The 2027 ICDC runs April 17–20 in Anaheim, California, and draws more than 25,000 students, advisors, and business professionals.

## Picking an event

Choose based on the career cluster that actually interests you, not the event that sounds most impressive. You'll spend months studying that cluster's material and practicing its scenarios, so genuine interest will carry you further than a prestigious-sounding name.

## The college admissions angle

Placing at a chartered association conference or at ICDC is a formal result from an outside organization, measured against a standard. That means it belongs in the Honors section of a college application, not just the Activities list. Being a DECA member is an activity. Placing at state or ICDC is an honor.

[CollegeIntel's explainer on DECA competitive events](https://collegeintel.fuselyn.com/articles/deca-competitive-events-explained) covers the formats and competition levels in more detail. If you're joining this year, pick your cluster early and practice both halves of your event separately. The exam and the roleplay reward different skills, and the students who place have trained for both.
